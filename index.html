The Horse

He won the first one he entered.

Started on the pattern by Lynn Kohr and campaigned by Kit McCall Johnson, Wolverrine walked into the first futurity of his life and left with the championship.

At the March Into Spring Fling in Shawnee, Oklahoma, Wolverrine topped a 62-horse futurity by nearly three-tenths on the aggregate — a margin that isn’t luck. It was worth $7,362 and it was the first futurity he and Johnson had ever entered together. Multiple futurity sidepot wins followed.

What the clock doesn’t show is the part breeders care about. He is honest in the turn, he is broke, and he kept his mind in a pen full of green horses and loud crowds. That disposition is the half of this horse his papers can’t promise you — and it is the half that makes him a horse you can hand to your daughter.

A buckskin out of a six-time NFR mare who can also be ridden by an amateur is not a marketing angle. It’s a very short list.

His Dam

Rosas Cantina CC

Six National Finals Rodeos. $677,168. The 2025 Daddy of ’Em All. And a produce record that already includes a Top 50 barrel sire.

Bred by Alan Woodbury of Woodbury Performance Horses in Dickinson, North Dakota, the 2010 buckskin mare by Corona Cartel and out of Dash Ta Vanila became one of the most recognizable horses in professional barrel racing under Lisa Lockhart — the first WPRA member to reach $4 million in career earnings.

At Cheyenne Frontier Days in 2025, Rosa made four clean runs through the hardest setup in rodeo and stopped the clock at 17.03 in the short round to win the championship and roughly $22,000.

Here is the part that matters to a breeder: Rosa does not just run. She produces. Twenty-five AQHA foals, $518,906 in progeny earnings, and Wolverrine’s half-brother Jaguarr — her son by Dash Ta Fame — ranked among the Top 50 barrel racing sires in 2025 off limited crops, after his own arena career was cut short.

A mare can be fast. A mare can be a producer. Rosa is both, and the market has already confirmed it with one son. Wolverrine is the other.

His Sire

The Goodbye Lane

More than $18 million in progeny earnings. No. 2 all-time among leading barrel racing sires. Twelve-time leading sire.

The 2001 brown stallion by Lanes Leinster and out of Swift Goodbye, by Dash Ta Fame, was bred and raced by Robert Morgan of Rigby, Idaho, and covered only a handful of mares before Mark Jarvis bought him in 2012. He has been rewriting the sire lists ever since from Jarvis Ranch in Spanish Fork, Utah.

He sired three of the top 10 all-time leading money earners — Force The Goodbye, who broke four round records at the 2024 NFR and won the world championship with $457,808 on the year, Hello Stella and MJ Segers Fast Lane. In 2025 alone his get earned $4,776,856 across 267 money earners. His five-year-olds brought home $1.9 million — double any other sire of five-year-olds in the country.

Which is the argument for breeding to a young son now. The Goodbye Lane is 25 years old. His sons are the access point.

Breeding

What ICSI actually means for you.

Wolverrine stands ICSI only. If that sentence is new to you, read this before you decide it’s out of reach — for most mare owners it costs less and asks less of the mare than a live cover season.

1

Your mare stays home

No hauling her to Texas, no layup bill, no missed season. A veterinarian aspirates eggs from your mare at a clinic near you. She can keep competing.

2

The lab does the work

A single Wolverrine sperm is injected directly into each egg. Because ICSI needs so little semen, one collection breeds many mares — which is exactly why the fee can stay where it is.

3

A recipient mare carries it

The embryo is transferred to a recipient mare and you get your foal without risking your good mare’s body or her year. Solo Select handles recipient mares and foaling in house.
